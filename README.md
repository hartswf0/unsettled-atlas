Unsettled Atlas

Synthetic places for testing how space becomes normal.

Unsettled Atlas turns spatial concepts into operable environments.

Orientation becomes terrain.
Power becomes access and friction.
History becomes substrate.
Relations become thresholds.
Description becomes operation.

A synthetic place is changed by being entered. Its instruments alter what they measure. Its boundaries reveal who maintains them. Every crossing changes both the traveler and the ground.

An unsettled place is not incomplete. Its conditions remain visible, contestable, and available for reconstruction.

The Soft Cadastre

A cadastre is the official register of land: who holds which parcel, in hard lines. The Soft Cadastre is the other one. It registers claimed extent — what people and institutions say about places — and holds every claim as bounded fuzzy ground that is never sharpened into a boundary it did not earn.

Two registries, one contract. SERES holds what individuals said, acquired from the OpenStreetMap Notes API. ATL NPU HALFWORLD holds what institutions claimed on the record, compiled from neighborhood planning minutes into kernel fields, claim lines, territory boxes and refusal walls.

Six laws govern both. Source anchor is not target. The compiler owns geometry, and no model ever emits a coordinate. Absence is not neutrality. The unresolved is kept and counted. LOOK is a gate a human must pass. Fixture declares itself.

Atlanta is the reference build; New Orleans tests what generalises. See soft-cadastre/SYSTEM.md.

The icosahedron's orientation and the eleven cuts that open the map are not transcribed from Fuller. They are solved from his criteria against coastline data — no vertex on land, cut where there is no land. See ICOSA-WORLD.md.

The solid is used twice. Above, the icosahedron holds the Earth. Turned toward discourse it is Stafford Beer's Team Syntegrity — thirty edges are thirty people, twelve vertices are twelve topics, everyone sits in two rooms, and closure is a property of the object rather than a promise about the chair. The compiler derives which solids can seat such a council, and proves two things the design rests on: a council cannot be scaled by subdividing the world, because every geodesic subdivision keeps exactly twelve five-valent vertices forever; and the icosahedron is the largest solid in which every pair of topics still shares a person. So a cell is entitled to a council by how much ground it holds — icosahedron at continental scale, octahedron at metropolitan, tetrahedron at block scale, and nothing at all below that.

A council is not a diagram beside the world. At planetary scale it is the world: the thirty struts are the thirty edges of the Dymaxion net and the twelve topics are its vertices, so unfolding scatters a council that folding gathers back into one. Inside a cell the topics sit on the corners and edge midpoints — the points a cell shares with its neighbours — so adjacent councils hold topics in common.

The seats are registers of description, not people. Nothing simulates a resident.

Icosa Syntegrity

Icosa World is a good map and an empty one: a cell had an id and an edge length and nothing else, which is why its council read as arbitrary and why nothing could be sent to anyone. Icosa Syntegrity is the fork where that is fixed. A triangle has corners in degrees, an area, a list of what is inside it, and an address that lives in the URL, so it can be linked and cited and returned to. The sheet sits beside the map instead of over it. A council seat is held by whoever types their name into it, and your critic is the seat opposite yours — a person, not a category. What is said stays on the triangle it was said about, drawn there, kept in the browser, and carried out as a file.

Descending reveals more rather than less: a compiled gazetteer carries 7,342 settlements with their populations and provinces, 1,366 named rivers, named regions and seas, airports, ports, and country outlines. So a triangle can say what is inside it and, more to the point, who a concern about it would be addressed to — sampled against the country outlines at its centre, corners and edge midpoints, with the provinces from the settlements within. When no state claims a triangle it says so.

The map cannot find the people in a small triangle and does not pretend to: it structures recruitment instead — an address you send, a seat someone claims by name, and a nomination for the next seat. An issue is a claim somebody seconded, so a concern reads as raised by one until it is held by two. What the map genuinely does is escalate: because a cell address is a prefix code, a concern raised on a block climbs to the scale at which a jurisdiction could answer it, and arrives there naming who that is.

Where the compiled gazetteer stops — at about a village — the page asks Wikipedia directly from the reader's browser, with no key and no server, and keeps only the articles that actually fall inside the triangle. And because a syntegration works by people carrying claims between the two rooms they each sit in, CARRY is an operation on a seat you hold, drawn on the map as a mark travelling the strut that moved it. A model may draft that carry from what is already in the room, citing every line, and may not send it.

The coastline is tiled: the 10m shore, lakes, built-up areas and major roads are clipped to each root face at build time and written as twenty files, so a reader downloads the ground they are looking at rather than the planet, and only once they descend past the scale where the coarse coastline is visibly wrong. Sea and land are painted as different things, which they had not been. And because a triangle is often the wrong unit — a watershed is several — PAINT drags across the map to mark a set of cells and keep it as a named area, which needs no new model because an area is just a set of addresses.

The links live on the map rather than beside it. A looked-up article is drawn where it actually is, as a real anchor with a live href and every affordance of being a link, and every painted mark is askable — tapping a city opens it with its province, population, coordinates, the triangle it sits in, and outbound links to Wikipedia and OpenStreetMap. Canvas text can be none of those things.

But links on the map only work while there are few of them. Fifty articles in a fourteen-kilometre triangle collided, stacked, and became a column down the middle of the world — covering the ground they pointed at, saying nothing about where anything was, and floating in mid-air the moment the world folded. The instrument already had an index and was not using it: the subdivision. A triangle holding more than a few finds now shows how many, tinted in proportion to what it holds, with the numeral painted into the triangle rather than sat in a box on top of it. A number is a door and a dot is a thing — go into a number and it breaks into four smaller numbers, go far enough and the numbers become names. Fifty articles are one numeral on the folded globe over Korea, because it is drawn on the face and folds with it. Binning is free: a find is addressed once, deep, and sorting it into a triangle is a prefix test on that address, the same trick escalation uses.

The panel draws the shape it is describing. It used to spend seven rows of text — ADDRESS, TOUCHES, INSIDE, CONTAINS, three CORNERs — on a triangle it never drew, and every one of those is a position rather than a list. So the cell is drawn at the top of it, oriented the way it is on screen right now, with the four children it actually has in the true subdivision and the three neighbours sitting across the edges they actually share, each carrying what it holds. A neighbour gets no digit, because a digit there would read as a fifth child. Everything in the picture is somewhere you can go, which answers at a glance what the rows answered nowhere: which way this triangle faces, which quarter is worth entering, and whether the interesting ground is next door instead. The breadcrumb is the same idea: a step down an address is a choice of one quarter out of four, so each step is drawn as that quarter and tapping it goes back. And a name in a panel that does not take you to what it names is just ink, so every row that stands for a place now moves the map to it and reads it there.

The convening runs in a harness, not a loop of chat calls: deterministic code decides what runs next, models only speak, score and write. The system instruction is itself a model output — a charter written once per ground from the compiled state and what is live there — and it becomes the hot prefix of every later call, with the per-call task riding at the very end of the context, so steering the tokens starts with deciding which ones repeat. Each topic team speaks, its critics answer, and then a judge — the other model of the same generation, where the account holds two, so the scorer is not the speaker marking its own work — scores every line: grounded, in-register, specific, engaged, each nought to three. A room below the gate is sent back once with the judge's notes and the better attempt is kept. Passing claims travel each seat's strut into its other room for the next reverberation, which is the actual edge topology of the solid doing the routing. Speakers may ask for the world and their questions are looked up between iterations. The run closes on an outcome statement per topic with dissent recorded, which is what the next convening inherits, and the whole thing is priced before the button and billed by stage after.

And it convenes. A syntegration is not a bench, it is a protocol: each topic team states its case and the seats whose registers are incommensurable with theirs answer, and because every seat sits in two rooms what is said in one arrives in the other. A round is two passes over every topic — the members speak from their registers and from the ground, then the critics answer, and the critics are given what was just said, because a critique written without hearing the statement is decoration. Three calls per topic per reverberation — speak, critique, judge — and the panel says the number before you press it, because it is not the file's money. Every line is written into the record stamped with the model, the round, the seat and its register, drawn on the map like anything else said there, readable as a transcript that says at the top what it is: a rehearsal of the argument this ground would have, not a record of one that happened.

And it is watchable without losing your place. The panel renders once when a run begins and is only patched afterwards — a single live region shows a breathing dot, the current stage, a call counter against the priced plan and the last three lines said, the newest sliding in — so wherever you have scrolled holds still, where the first convening bounced the view to the top on every one of its thirty-eight steps. The map plays the same run at the other scale: an expanding ring at the topic point of the room now speaking, dashed ink while its judge scores, a pulse at the cell's centre for the charter and the outcomes that belong to no single room, and when a room passes the gate its carries visibly travel — small signals running the struts from the topic to each seat's other room, staggered so simultaneous carries read as traffic. The reverberation, drawn as itself.

The run also leaves as a prompt, not a printout: COPY THE RUN puts one document on the clipboard written to be given to another model and executed — it binds itself to the polygon by address, corners and containment chain, makes the receiver the harness in six numbered steps (fetch the people and the live issues first if the bench or agenda is thin, adopt the included system instruction verbatim, convene the reverberations, judge, carry along the struts, close on outcomes), embeds everything already fetched as state, and demands its own transcript format back with the address on it, so the run stays tracked to its triangle in whoever's context window it lands. Given to a person it reads as the complete record of the last convening; TAKE IT AS A FILE saves it as markdown. And the slow parts of asking the record got faster without getting ruder: a host still throttling after the lane has slowed down twice is rested so later callers fail fast to the fallback instead of each repeating the retry cycle, readership is asked for once per recruit rather than once per rung, and opening a council quietly warms the summary cache for its topics and largest settlements so the card that opens on a tap already knows its text.

They argue from what is live rather than what is historical. GDELT searched by the names of the settlements the triangle actually contains, and the recent revisions of the Wikipedia articles inside it — what people are editing about a place is a real signal of what is unresolved there — both landing in the compiled context marked with where they came from. And the bill is not a footnote: every call's usage is counted, broken down by operation, kept in the record, with the cached-input column shown separately, because the context barely changes between calls on one cell and if that stops being a cache read it should be visible rather than arriving later as a number.

The model is an instrument of the triangle rather than a textbox hidden in the council. It is called through the Responses API — instructions separate from input, structured output by strict JSON Schema, reasoning effort where the model takes it, no temperature at all — and the model list comes from `/v1/models` on your own account rather than a name baked into the source, so a new generation appears without a source edit. The ranking is parsed rather than looked up: the number in the id orders the generations and the tier word orders within one, so GPT-5.6's named tiers — sol at the frontier, terra balanced, luna fast and cheap — sort correctly, and so will whatever ships next. Reasoning controls follow what the generation takes, with pro mode and max effort offered only on the frontier tier that has them, and no temperature sent to any of them, because these are reasoning models and it is not a knob they have. It is given the compiled world state, `icosa-context-v2`: the address, what contains it and what it touches, the ground, the council with its topic topology and who answers whom and where every seat's name came from, what has been said and carried and escalated, and the evidence with its provenance. The pipeline is triangle → retrieve → assemble context → reason → structured proposal → a human accepts → record, and no server-side conversation memory is used, because the memory should be recompiled from the local record every call. The panel shows what the model is allowed to know before anything is asked, and prints the whole object on request. There is one field: paste a key. Everything else is chosen — the best model the account holds, a balanced reasoning effort, the whole compiled world state — and it runs the moment the council opens, so it is zero clicks from a key to a full bench. The dials are folded away rather than removed, because a thing you cannot inspect is a thing you have to trust. The key persists under its own name in local storage, never written into the record and scrubbed from every export; it used to live on the record and leave inside it, which is the one path the record exists for.

A triangle used to come back empty because of the query, not the ground: Wikipedia's geosearch caps its radius at ten kilometres, so a 441 km triangle was being answered by a disc covering a twentieth of one per cent of it. Wikidata's query service has no such cap, so it is the primary source now — `wikibase:around` at any radius, ranked by language editions, one query where three used to chain. Below it: geosearch again but sampled at up to ten points across the triangle rather than only its middle, then OpenStreetMap for every named settlement in the box, then the gazetteer compiled into the page, which cannot fail. Four rungs, no key, no account. The fifth is a key of yours, for the one thing no database holds — the statements of importance, which is where a syntegration actually starts — and everything it produces is stamped as inferred, dashed and grey, with a link that is honestly a search rather than a source.

The seats fill from the record. A council of empty seats is a diagram rather than a body, so the bench is drawn from Wikidata — the places inside the triangle, then the humans born, died or working at any of them, ranked by a stated heuristic: how local they are first, then how many language editions carry them, then how much they are read. Locality is weighted heaviest, because a council for this ground that returns the same six global names at every scale is not a council for this ground. Geometry does the seating: every seat is a strut between two topics, and a person goes to the seat whose midpoint their tied place is nearest. It never comes back empty at any scale — a cell address is a prefix code, so if a fourteen-kilometre triangle knows nobody the question climbs to its parent and to its parent until it is answered, every name carries the scale it was found at so a borrowed one is visibly borrowed, and with no network at all a seat still reads what ground it speaks for, because a topic is a real point and the gazetteer names it offline. Nothing has to be clicked: opening a council asks the record immediately, the seats claim themselves when it answers, and the topics stop being coordinates and become subjects — the most-read article at each topic point, or the nearest named thing the gazetteer knows when the network says nothing, so the twelve planetary topics that all sit at sea still come back named. Every row says which kind of full it is. A seat filled from the record reads "from the record · found on this ground · 215 languages · 4,100,000 reads · not a person who agreed to sit"; a seat somebody typed reads "claimed by hand". Type over a filled seat and it becomes yours. That stamp is what makes filling them automatically honest rather than laundering a citation into a participant.

Everything here sticks, and everything can be let go. A triangle stays picked, a trace stays drawn, a card stays open — and there was no way back to nothing except a key a phone does not have, because tapping the map only ever picked something else. Now tapping the triangle you already have releases it, so the way out is the same gesture as the way in, and when the map is holding anything at all a DROP button appears naming what it will let go of, peeling one layer at a time with painted work last, because that is the only thing here somebody actually made.

And it is read where it is. Wikipedia cannot be framed — en.wikipedia.org sends X-Frame-Options, so an iframe renders blank — but its summary endpoint answers cross-origin, so the article's first paragraph is drawn in a card beside the dot rather than raising a full-height sheet and then throwing you off the page. The card belongs to the point and not to the screen: a leader runs from the mark to the card, panning carries it along with the ground, and taking the point off the world closes it. On a phone it covers about a third of the screen where the sheet covered all of it.

The compiled gazetteer stops at about a village. Below roughly five kilometres a triangle divides the ground without telling you more about it, and the panel says so rather than pretending. See ICOSA-SYNTEGRITY.md.

Worldtext

An address-first world language, kept in worldtext/ and ready to be split into its own repository. Faces were already addressable — @F/09/3/2 is a path down the subdivision — but edges and vertices were not, and that is the trap: two faces share an edge, so naming it from each side invents two names for one thing and every claim attached to it splits in half.

So a vertex is addressed by integer barycentric position inside a base face, @V/07/3.2.3, reduced while every coordinate is even and canonicalised to the smallest face that can see it. An edge is its two vertices in canonical order, so EDGE(A,B) and EDGE(B,A) are one string. Euler's formula checks the whole scheme at once: at every depth each edge is named by exactly two faces, E = 3F/2, and V − E + F = 2.

The payoff is that assigning a person to an edge hands them their situation with no role machinery at all — two endpoint convergences and two adjacent faces, straight out of the geometry. See worldtext/README.md.
