Abyssinian Highlands

Seven windows on the Ethiopian Highlands and the Blue Nile, each 12 km square, seen by Sentinel-2 at 10 m per pixel. Each can be turned into 3D terrain. Yellow outlines are machine-drawn field units from the open Fields of the World map, each window with a verdict on whether they hold up. Yosemite Valley sits at the end of the row as the yardstick for relief. Ahiya on Real Ground sets the DONKEY WATER circuit on the Simien window.

Are the outlines real? Three checks per window

On cropland: share of outlined area that ESA WorldCover 2021, a separate map, calls cropland. Seen again: share of 2025 units (0.1 ha or larger) that the separate 2024 run drew in nearly the same place; a displaced copy scores about 1%. Uniform inside: how much more even the image is inside a unit than inside the same shape moved 70 m, on two dates; 1.0 means no better than chance. A fourth check, whether outlines sit on visible edges, scored 0.94–1.13 everywhere, so the outlines do not trace field edges at this resolution.

What is measured and what is not. Imagery: Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A, one brightness stretch for all three bands so colours are not shifted. Elevation: Copernicus GLO-30. Field units: Fields of the World global predictions for 2025 (Robinson et al. 2026, CC BY 4.0), a model run on 10 m imagery. Ethiopia is outside the area where that model reports a confidence score, so every polygon here has none. A field unit is a patch the model saw as one field, not a legal or surveyed plot. Coordinates marked "given" came with the brief; "chosen" and "moved" ones were picked for this atlas.
